# Cap Collective — Accessibility & Inclusion Guide
### Make Sure Every Student Can Fully Participate
**Physical, sensory, cognitive, language, economic, and cultural inclusion — with a practical audit checklist.**

---

# PART 1: PRINCIPLES
1. **Universal Design:** design roles so the *default* works for the widest range of students.
2. **Multiple paths to participate:** if one role is inaccessible, there's always another.
3. **Dignity first:** never single a student out or "help" without consent.
4. **Ask, don't assume:** each student knows their needs — involve them and their support staff.
5. **Aligned with existing plans:** integrate with IEPs/504s, not around them.

---

# PART 2: PHYSICAL / MOBILITY

## Barrier → Fix
| Barrier | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| High workbenches | Height-adjustable table or sturdy platform; seated workstation |
| Standing-only stations | Provide stool/chairs; allow seated washing/sorting |
| Reach | Keep frequently-used tools within 40-120 cm; store light items low |
| Heavy caps bags | Use a rolling cart; two-person lift rule; smaller containers |
| Narrow aisles | Keep 90 cm+ clearance (also a fire-code win) |
| Long standing | Rotate roles every 15-20 min |
| Grip difficulty | Lever-style tools, jigs/clamps, universal cuffs, electric scissors |

## Accessible Station Design
```
Collect:  seated drop station + wide slot
Sort:     seated table, tray at lap height, bright light
Wash:     seated basin, warm water, long-handle brush
Press:    operated by certified adult OR accessible jig for upper-body push
Store:    counter-height display, card reader reachable
```

# PART 3: SENSORY & NEURODIVERSITY

**The workshop is loud, hot, bright, and smelly. Plan for it.**

| Need | Accommodation |
|------|---------------|
| Noise sensitivity | Ear defenders (not just plugs); quiet role option; advance warning before machines start |
| Light sensitivity | Cap on laser glare; switch to non-flammable task lighting; warning before laser fires |
| Smell sensitivity | Respirator by preference; position non-melt roles upwind/away; air out room |
| Heat sensitivity | Breaks; water; do melt tasks in cooler part of day |
| Tactile defensiveness | Gloves by preference (not force); tongs instead of hand contact |
| Fidget/regulation | Fidget-friendly roles (sorting caps is calming); movement breaks |
| Focus differences | Visual checklists, timers, one-step-at-a-time cards |
| Predictability | Posted schedule; a "what's happening today" visual |

**Quiet role ladder:** Data entry → Sorting → Washing → Design → Assembly. Melt/machine roles are highest-sensory — offer alternatives, never require.

**Sensory kit to keep stocked:** ear defenders, sunglasses, unscented wipes, a calm corner, timers, visual schedule.

# PART 4: COGNITIVE & LEARNING

| Support | How |
|---------|-----|
| Short instructions | Show one step at a time (photo cards) |
| Reading level | Use visuals, icons, pictograms; provide audio versions |
| Memory | Laminated SOP cards at each station |
| Attention | Role rotation; short tasks; visible timers |
| Executive function | Checklists; partner system; clear "done" signals |
| Difficulty understanding | Buddy mentor; demonstration over explanation |

**Make visual SOPs:** each station gets a 3-4 picture card (see `05_PRINTABLE_RESOURCES.md`).

# PART 5: LANGUAGE (EAL / ELL)
- Bilingual labels for plastics (#2 HDPE / #5 PP) and safety.
- Picture-first instructions.
- Key vocabulary cards: *sort, shred, melt, mold, certify* — with images.
- Group EAL students with patient partners; avoid relying on fast speech.
- Offer the safety quiz in translation where available; or read it aloud.

---

# PART 6: ECONOMIC INCLUSION
**Don't let "bring caps from home" exclude students who can't.**
- Provide a **supply of caps onsite** so everyone can participate.
- Never rank by "caps brought from home" — rank by **caps processed together**.
- Provide snacks/water at long sessions; cover any costs (transport for trips).
- Eco-Store: offer a "free for those who need it" option discreetly.
- Avoid requiring purchases (no "you must buy a shirt").

---

# PART 7: GENDER & BELONGING IN MAKING
- Recruit girls/non-binary students as **machine operators and leads**, not only sorters.
- Name and rotate leadership; don't let one group monopolize machines.
- Show diverse role models (women engineers, makers).
- Language: "makers," "engineers," "designers" — not gendered.
- Watch participation data by group; act if a group is excluded.

---

# PART 8: CULTURAL & RELIGIOUS INCLUSION
- Respect dietary/kosher/halal considerations if food is involved at events.
- Avoid scheduling heavy work over religious observances.
- Reflect students' cultures in product designs and marketing imagery.
- Solicit input on celebrations and campaigns.

---

# PART 9: DIGITAL ACCESSIBILITY (Dashboard & Materials)
- Documents: use headings, alt text, high contrast; provide PDF + text.
- Dashboard: colorblind-safe palettes; don't rely on color alone.
- Videos: **captions** (auto + check); transcripts; no flashing.
- QR codes: provide text URLs too.
- Ensure materials open on phones (many students' only device).

---

# PART 10: SUPPORT STAFF & IEP INTEGRATION
- Invite **case managers / LSAs / EAs** to PD; give them the role list.
- Map roles to IEP goals (e.g., fine-motor → sorting; social → recruiting).
- Document accommodations used and share with the team.
- If a student needs a 1:1, coordinate supervision for machine roles.

# PART 12: WHEN YOU GET IT WRONG
If a student is excluded or uncomfortable:
1. **Listen** and believe them.
2. **Fix immediately** (change the role/station).
3. **Document** what happened and the fix.
4. **Adjust the system** so it doesn't recur.
5. **Follow up** with the student and their support team.
