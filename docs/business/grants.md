# PART A: GRANT APPLICATION TEMPLATES

## TEMPLATE 1: GENERAL FOUNDATION GRANT (Any Region)

### 1. PROJECT TITLE
**Cap Collective: A Student-Led Circular Economy & STEM Program at [School Name]**

### 2. PROJECT SUMMARY (150 words)
Cap Collective is a student-run circular economy program that diverts HDPE bottle cap waste from landfill while teaching STEM, entrepreneurship, and environmental stewardship. Students collect caps on campus, process them through a 5-step cycle (Collect → Clean → Shred → Melt → Refine), and manufacture saleable products such as coasters, desk organizers, and plant pots. All revenue funds the school garden and program sustainability.

In Year 1, [School Name] will engage [50-300] students, divert [10,000+] caps ([35+] kg) from landfill, avoid [65+] kg CO₂e, and generate [$500+] in product revenue — with every student certified in safe machine operation. The program is curriculum-aligned, replicable, and open-source, positioning [School Name] as a regional leader in sustainability education.

### 3. STATEMENT OF NEED (250 words)
**The problem:** Globally, only 3% of bottle caps are recycled. Bottle caps are among the top five deadliest marine debris items, frequently ingested by wildlife because of their small size and bright colors. Even where recycling exists, caps are too small for industrial sorting machinery and fall through the cracks — ending up in landfill or oceans.

**The local gap:** [School Name] currently sends approximately [__] kg of mixed plastic waste to general waste each term. Caps are mixed with bottles, contaminating recycling streams and losing value. Students witness this waste daily but lack a system to act — a gap between *intent* and *impact*.

**Why us:** Our school is a high-consumption hub with [__] students. A single lunch hour generates [__] plastic bottles. We have the space, the staff commitment, and the student interest to convert this liability into an asset. What we lack is seed funding for equipment and safety infrastructure — the exact barrier this grant removes.

**Why now:** [School Name] is expanding its STEM and sustainability curriculum. Cap Collective offers a ready-made, standards-aligned anchor unit that turns a school-wide environmental problem into a hands-on learning and revenue opportunity.

### 4. PROGRAM DESIGN (300 words)
**Phase 1 — Launch (Weeks 1-2):** Secure administrative approval and a ventilated workspace; procure equipment; recruit program lead and student team; sign safety MOU.

**Phase 2 — Collect (Weeks 3-4):** Map high-traffic collection zones; build and deploy [5+] laser-cut collection boxes with IR sensors; launch during school assembly; complete student safety certification.

**Phase 3 — Process (Weeks 5-8):** Run weekly cycles — collect, wash, shred, press, finish. Track all data in the Command Center dashboard. Produce first product batch.

**Phase 4 — Product (Weeks 9-12):** Run a design sprint; design molds in Fusion 360; produce three product lines; implement student-led quality control.

**Phase 5 — Market (Weeks 13-15):** Launch the student-run Eco-Store; host a community recycling event; publish first public impact report.

**Phase 6 — Scale (Weeks 16-18):** Document and open-source the model; train the next cohort; submit follow-on grant applications.

**Safety:** All machine operations require certification (video + quiz + supervised demo + signed contract). Ratio: 1 certified adult per 4 students. Ventilation, PPE, and temperature limits are mandatory and audited termly.

**Assessment:** Students are assessed on NGSS/Common Core/IB standards via products, data reports, and presentations (rubric provided).

### 5. OUTCOMES & MEASUREMENT (200 words)
| Outcome | Target (Year 1) | Measurement |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| Caps diverted | 10,000+ units | IR sensor + manual audit |
| Waste diverted | 35+ kg | Weekly weight logs |
| CO₂e avoided | 65+ kg | EPA WARM model |
| Students engaged | [50-300] | Role sign-ups |
| Students certified | [25+] | Certification log |
| Product revenue | $500+ | Command Center P&L |
| Products manufactured | 200+ units | Inventory tracker |
| Community events | 2+ | Event log |
| Grant follow-ons submitted | 1+ | Documentation |

Data is tracked live in a public dashboard, ensuring transparency and enabling replication.

### 6. BUDGET (attach Command Center procurement sheet)
| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Heat press (budget tier) | $350 |
| Hand tools & PPE | $250 |
| Collection box materials | $150 |
| Shredder | $220 |
| Sensor kits (ESP32 + IR ×5) | $50 |
| Consumables (parchment, soap, bags) | $100 |
| **Total Request** | **$1,120** |

*(Adjust to your tier. FabLab/Center budgets available on request.)*

### 7. SUSTAINABILITY (150 words)
Cap Collective is designed to outlive its grant. Product sales generate ongoing revenue (60-85% margins) that funds consumables and equipment maintenance. The program becomes a permanent, student-managed fixture of the school's STEM curriculum. Documentation is open-source, allowing other schools to replicate at near-zero cost. Over three years, we project self-sufficiency through a combination of product revenue, school budget line items, and reinvestment of savings on waste management.

### 8. EVALUATION (150 words)
We will evaluate success using the metrics in Section 5, reviewed monthly by the Program Lead and termly by leadership. Student learning is assessed against NGSS/Common Core/IB standards using the provided rubric. We will gather qualitative feedback via student surveys and staff reflection. All results are compiled into public impact reports and shared with the funder at [quarterly/annual] intervals.

### 9. CONCLUSION
Cap Collective turns a daily environmental problem into a measurable educational and economic asset. With [grant amount], [School Name] will divert [10,000+] caps, certify [50+] students, and build a self-sustaining program that serves as a regional model. *This is not a one-time project — it is infrastructure for a zero-waste campus economy.*
