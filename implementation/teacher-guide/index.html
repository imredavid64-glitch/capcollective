    Cap Collective — Teacher Implementation Guide¶

    The Complete Playbook for Running a Student-Led Plastic Recycling Program¶

    Version 1.0 | For Teachers, Club Advisors & Makerspace Coordinators | ~50 pages equivalent

    HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE¶

    This guide is organized so you can start in Week 0 and follow along sequentially, or jump to any chapter as a reference. Each chapter ends with a "Do This Now" checklist.

    Chapter Read When Time to Implement
    1. Getting Started Before you commit 2-3 hours
    2. Curriculum Mapping Planning phase 4-6 hours
    3. Operations Playbook Week 1-2 1 week setup
    4. Equipment & Safety Before equipment arrives 4 hours + PD
    5. Product Development Week 9 2 weeks
    6. Data & Impact Week 3 onward 3 hours
    7. Community & Fundraising Week 13 Ongoing
    8. Appendices As needed Reference

    CHAPTER 1: GETTING STARTED (Week 0-1)¶

    1.1 The 5-Minute Pitch (For Admin)¶

    "Cap Collective is a student-run circular economy program. Students collect HDPE bottle caps from campus, process them into plastic sheets and molded products using safe, small-scale machinery, and sell those products to fund the school garden. It hits STEM standards, entrepreneurship, data science, and sustainability — all in one program. We can start for under $1,000."

    Why administrations say yes: - Low floor, high ceiling — start Core tier, scale to FabLab - Standards-aligned — not a "fun extra," it's curriculum - Revenue-generating — products fund other programs - Measurable impact — dashboards prove value for grants/PR - Student-led — builds leadership, not teacher workload

    1.2 Admin Buy-In Checklist¶

    • 30-min presentation (use Launch Deck, Slides 1-24)
    • Identify Program Lead (a teacher with 2-4 hrs/week)
    • Confirm budget tier (Core / FabLab / Center)
    • Confirm space (see 1.3)
    • Sign Safety MOU (Appendix A)
    • Add to school calendar (launch week, PD dates)

    1.3 Space Assessment¶

    You need a room that satisfies ALL of these:

    Requirement Why Minimum Spec
    Ventilation Plastic off-gassing Window + exhaust fan (100+ CFM) OR fume hood
    Power Heat press + shredder draw 1× 16A circuit (US: 20A), grounded
    Water Washing station Sink within 20m, or use basins
    Surface Work tables 3× tables, heat-resistant mats
    Storage Caps, flakes, products Lockable cabinets (chemicals/blades)
    Floor Safety Non-slip, easy-clean

    If you don't have a dedicated room: Use the art room, science lab, or makerspace on a rotating schedule. The heat press can be on a rolling cart.

    1.4 Choosing Your Tier¶

    CORE ($500-1,000) — Start Here¶

    Includes: Budget heat press (Transmax/Microtec), hand tools, PPE, DIY wooden boxes, manual shredder. Produces: Coasters, simple sheets, keychains (basic). Best for: Primary schools, after-school clubs, pilots.

    FABLAB ($2,500-5,000) — Scale Up¶

    Adds: Auto-open press (Secabo TC5), Bambu Lab A1 / Prusa MK4S, Arduino sensor kits, GDPR shredder, basic CNC. Produces: Full product line + custom molds + 3D filament. Best for: Middle/High, makerspaces, STEM academies.

    CENTER ($10,000+) — District Hub¶

    Adds: Premium press (Stahls), injection molder (SDS Injection Mini 2), Trotec laser, precision aluminum molds, industrial ventilation. Produces: Architectural tiles, B2B orders, filament at scale. Best for: Technical colleges, district resource centers.

    1.5 DO THIS NOW¶

    • Present to admin this week
    • Complete space assessment (walk the room, photograph it)
    • Get tier approved in writing
    • Email procurement (see Chapter 4 supplier list)

    CHAPTER 2: CURRICULUM MAPPING (Planning Phase)¶

    2.1 The Philosophy¶

    Cap Collective is designed as an interdisciplinary anchor unit. Rather than "finding time," it replaces or enhances existing lessons. A single 6-week cycle can cover 15+ standards.

    2.2 NGSS Alignment (US)¶

    Standard Grade Cap Collective Activity
    K-ESS3-3 K Communicate solutions to reduce human impact (cap collection)
    2-PS1-1 2 Classify materials by observable properties (cap sorting)
    3-5-ETS1-1 3-5 Define a design problem (product design)
    5-PS1-3 5 Measure/identify materials by properties (HDPE vs PET)
    MS-PS1-3 6-8 Gather info on synthetic materials' impact (LCA)
    MS-ETS1-2 6-8 Evaluate competing design solutions (mold design)
    HS-PS2-6 9-12 Communicate why material properties matter (polymer science)
    HS-ETS1-3 9-12 Optimize a design solution (production efficiency)

    2.3 IB Alignment (MYP/DP)¶

    • MYP Design: Criterion A (Inquiry), B (Developing ideas), C (Creating solution), D (Evaluating)
    • MYP Sciences: Criterion C (Processing/evaluating data), D (Reflecting on impacts)
    • DP ESS: Topic 8 (Human systems & resource use), Internal Assessment
    • DP CAS: Creativity, Activity, Service — perfect fit

    2.4 Common Core (Math/ELA)¶

    • Math: Ratios (yield), statistics (collection data), geometry (design), modeling
    • ELA: Technical writing (SOPs), persuasive writing (grants), presentations

    2.5 Lesson Plans by Grade Band¶

    K-2: "Cap Detectives" (3 lessons)¶

    Lesson 1: What Are Plastic Caps? (45 min) - Hook: Show caps vs. bottles. "They're different!" - Activity: Sort a pile of caps by color/size/texture - Standard: K-ESS3-3, 2-PS1-1 - Assessment: Sorting worksheet (in Appendix)

    Lesson 2: Where Does a Cap Go? (45 min) - Hook: Story "Cap's Journey" (included printable) - Activity: Sequence cards (bottle → bin → ocean OR bottle → box → coaster) - Assessment: Sequencing worksheet

    Lesson 3: Count & Graph (45 min) - Activity: Count this week's caps, make a class bar graph - Extension: Predict next week's count

    3-5: "Cap Engineers" (3 lessons)¶

    Lesson 4: Build the Box (90 min, double period) - Build a collection box from laser-cut kit - Math: Measure, assemble, check square - Standard: 3-5-ETS1-1

    Lesson 5: From Caps to Coaster (90 min) - Watch press cycle (teacher demo) - Students design a coaster in Tinkercad - Standard: 3-5-ETS1-2

    Lesson 6: The Numbers (45 min) - Weigh flakes, calculate yield, graph - Standard: 5-PS1-3, Math 5.MD

    6-8: "Cap Innovators" (3 lessons)¶

    Lesson 7: Programming the Counter (90 min) - Wire ESP32 + IR sensor, upload code - Test with falling caps - Standard: MS-ETS1-2

    Lesson 8: Life Cycle Assessment (90 min) - Research virgin vs recycled HDPE impact - Build comparison dashboard - Standard: MS-PS1-3

    Lesson 9: The Business Case (90 min) - Cost a product (material + labor + overhead) - Set price, calculate margin, pitch to "investors" - Standard: MS-ETS1-2

    9-12: "Cap Systems Designers" (3 lessons)¶

    Lesson 10: Polymer Science & Rheology (90 min) - Test melt flow, measure shrinkage, document - Standard: HS-PS2-6

    Lesson 11: Systems Optimization (90 min) - Map full workflow, find bottleneck, propose fix - Standard: HS-ETS1-3

    Lesson 12: Grant Writing & Scale (2 periods) - Draft a real grant (template provided) - Present to "board" (panel of staff) - Standard: ELA W.9-10.1

    2.6 Interdisciplinary Connections¶

    Subject Connection
    Art Product design, branding, color mixing of flakes
    Math Yield %, cost per unit, data analysis
    CS Sensor programming, dashboards, automation
    Business Pricing, marketing, inventory, sales
    Language Technical writing, presentations, marketing copy
    Social Studies Circular economy, policy, global supply chains
    Engineering Mold design, machine building, QC

    2.7 DO THIS NOW¶

    • Select your 3 anchor lessons for your grade band
    • Map to your local standards (edit the tables)
    • Book double periods for build/machine days
    • Copy lesson resources to your LMS

    3.2 Zone Mapping (Do This First)¶

    Walk your campus with students. Score each location:

    Location Traffic (1-5) Cap Likelihood (1-5) Accessible? Score Priority
    Cafeteria entrance 5 5 Yes 25 1
    Gym door 5 3 Yes 15 3
    Main entry 4 2 Yes 8 5
    Library 3 2 Yes 6 6
    Near vending 4 5 Yes 20 2
    Sports fields 2 4 Yes 8 5

    Rule of thumb: 1 box per 150 students, prioritizing top 3 scores.

    3.3 Box Building (Class Activity)¶

    Materials per box: - Laser-cut plywood kit (cad/collection_box.scad → export DXF; final CAD to be added) - Wood glue (1 bottle per 3 boxes) - Clamps (4+) - Acrylic front panel - IR sensor + ESP32 (FabLab tier) - Signage (printed)

    Assembly Steps (from Instructables): 1. Glue side edges. 2. Position and attach pieces. 3. Hold panels to set (60 seconds). 4. Repeat to finish structural box. 5. Install sensor slot & acrylic. 6. Mount signage.

    Time: 30-45 min/box with 2 students. Tip: A flush box ensures IR sensors work perfectly.

    3.4 Collection Protocol¶

    • Frequency: Weekly (Fridays) minimum; daily in high-traffic zones
    • Responsible: Collection Captain + rotating volunteers
    • Steps:
    • Empty box into labeled tote (by color if possible)
    • Weigh tote (record grams)
    • Photograph for dashboard
    • Replace box, wipe if needed
    • Log in Command Center → Collection Tracker tab

    3.5 Cleaning Protocol (CRITICAL)¶

    This is the most important step for quality.

    1. Sort: HDPE #2 vs PP #5 (check underside symbol)
    2. Remove liners: Pull out silicone/foam inner seals (they don't melt!)
    3. Soak: Warm water + dish soap, 10 min
    4. Scrub: Old toothbrush for stubborn residue
    5. Rinse: Until water runs clear
    6. Dry: Air dry 24 hours on mesh (no towel — lint!)
    7. QC: Any cap with adhesive/paper/liquid smell → reject

    Why: Residual milk/sugar burns at high temp → toxic fumes + discolored product.

    3.6 Shredding SOP (Grade 6+, Certified)¶

    1. Clear blades of residual plastic
    2. Toggle motor, verify rotation
    3. Feed slowly — do not overfeed (stalls motor)
    4. Target flake size: 5-8mm
    5. Sieve through mesh screen (consistency)
    6. Store in labeled buckets by color
    7. Oil blades after each session

    3.7 Heat Press SOP (Grade 6+, Certified)¶

    HDPE Settings: 140°C | PP Settings: 170°C

    1. Turn on press, set temp & time
    2. Wait for platen to reach FULL temp (verify with IR thermometer)
    3. Parchment sandwich: flakes between two baking-paper sheets
    4. Press 5 min → flip → press 5 min
    5. Cool under weight (prevents warping)
    6. Remove, measure thickness

    ⚠️ WARNING: Never let plastic touch the metal platen. Monitor constantly — thermal runaway is possible.

    3.8 Role Rotation Schedule¶

    Rotate every 2 weeks so all students experience multiple roles.

    Week Collection Wash Shred Press QC Data
    1-2 Group A Group B Group C Group D Group E Group F
    3-4 Group B Group C Group D Group E Group F Group A
    5-6 ... ... ... ... ... ...

    Space Type Minimum Recommended
    Classroom 100 CFM exhaust 200 CFM + HEPA
    Makerspace 250 CFM Fume hood + 400 CFM
    Dedicated lab Fume hood Full HVAC + monitoring

    4.4 PPE Checklist (Per Student at Machines)¶

    • Safety goggles (ANSI Z87.1)
    • Heat-resistant gloves (rated 500°F / 260°C)
    • VOC respirator (N95 minimum; P100 for frequent use)
    • Closed-toe shoes
    • Hair tied back, no loose sleeves
    • No dangling jewelry

    4.5 Machine Safety Certification¶

    Every student must complete before machine access: 1. Watch safety video (included) 2. Pass 10-question quiz (80% to pass) 3. Supervised practical demo 4. Sign Student Safety Contract (Appendix B) 5. Receive Certification Card (color-coded by machine)

    Certification Logger: Command Center → Tab 5

    4.6 Risk Assessment (Completed Example)¶

    Hazard Likelihood Severity Controls Residual
    Burns from press Medium High Gloves, temp monitoring, no-touch rule, supervision Low
    Fumes Low High Ventilation, respirators, temp limits Low
    Cuts from blades Medium Medium Guards, training, first aid Low
    Electrical Low High 16A circuit, RCD, no water near machines Low
    Fire Low High Extinguisher, fire blanket, temp limits Low

    5.3 Mold Design (Fusion 360)¶

    1. Design part (student CAD)
    2. Create two-part mold with gates (plastic entry) and vents (air escape)
    3. Fabricate: CNC mill or 3D print
    4. Apply release agent before use

    Key principle: Design mono-material products (single polymer) so they can be re-shredded.

    5.4 Quality Control Standards¶

    See Launch Deck Slide 27. Every batch gets a QC sheet (Appendix C).

    Rejection causes & fixes: | Defect | Cause | Fix | |--------|-------|-----| | Voids/bubbles | Uneven flake size | Sieve more, pre-mix | | Warping | Cooled too fast | Cool under weight | | Burn marks | Temp too high | Lower to 140°C, monitor | | Weak product | Mixed polymers | Sort strictly by #2/#5 | | Rough edges | No sanding | Sand with 120→400 grit |

    5.5 Packaging & Branding¶

    • Brand: "Cap Collective [School Name]"
    • Label: Batch #, material %, "Made from X caps diverted"
    • Story: Include a card: "This product was made from caps collected at [school]."
    • Sustainability: Kraft paper, no plastic!

    5.6 DO THIS NOW¶

    • Run design sprint (student teams)
    • Select 3 products for launch
    • Make molds / laser files
    • Print QC sheets
    • Design labels & packaging

    CHAPTER 6: DATA & IMPACT MEASUREMENT (Week 3+)¶

    6.1 The Command Center¶

    The included Google Sheets workbook auto-calculates: - Running totals (caps, weight, value) - Diversion rate (% of caps kept from landfill) - CO₂e avoided (EPA WARM conversion) - Participation % and leaderboards

    See 03_COMMAND_CENTER_SHEETS.md for full structure.

    6.2 Sensor Setup (FabLab+)¶

    Hardware: ESP32 + IR beam-break sensor Wiring: 

    IR Sensor VCC → ESP32 3.3V
IR Sensor GND → ESP32 GND
IR Sensor OUT → ESP32 GPIO 27
IR Sensor on chute opposite a small IR LED (or use reflective)
    Logic: When beam breaks → increment counter → send to dashboard via WiFi/HTTP.

    Code: Included in Resources (sensor_count.ino).

    6.3 Dashboard Options¶

    Tool Skill Level Cost Best For
    Google Sheets Low Free All grades
    Looker Studio Medium Free 6-12, public dashboards
    Vercel + Next.js High Free 9-12, custom
    PowerBI Medium Licensed Districts

    6.4 Reporting Templates¶

    Monthly Report (1 page): - Caps collected (units + weight) - Products made - Revenue - Participation - 1 student quote - Photo

    Semester Report (3 pages): - All above + trend charts - CO₂e avoided - Lessons learned - Next term goals

    Annual Report (for grants): - Full year data - Financial statement - Impact narrative - Testimonials - Scaling plan

    6.5 CO₂e Calculation¶

    CO₂e avoided = (mass of HDPE diverted in kg) × 1.9 kg CO₂e/kg
Source: EPA WARM model (conservative estimate for HDPE recycling vs landfill)
    Example: 35 kg diverted × 1.9 = 66.5 kg CO₂e avoided ≈ 165 miles not driven.

    6.6 DO THIS NOW¶

    • Wire & test first sensor (or start manual)
    • Build dashboard (Sheets → Looker Studio)
    • Schedule monthly reports

    Appendix E: Troubleshooting FAQ¶

    Problem Solution
    Caps won't melt evenly Sieve flakes; ensure uniform size
    Product warps Cool under weight; slow cooling
    Strong smell Lower temp; check ventilation; clean caps
    Sensor miscounts Adjust beam alignment; add debounce code
    Shredder jams Feed slower; clear blades; don't overfeed
    Mold sticks Apply release agent; cool longer
    Low participation Add competition; show dashboard publicly
    No admin support Use revenue + standards angle in Launch Deck
    Equipment breaks See warranty; keep spare parts list
    Grant rejected Ask for feedback; reapply; use templates

    Appendix F: Glossary¶

    • HDPE: High-Density Polyethylene (Plastic #2) — caps
    • PP: Polypropylene (Plastic #5) — lids, cups
    • PET: Polyethylene Terephthalate (Plastic #1) — bottles
    • Flake: Shredded plastic pieces (5-8mm)
    • Feedstock: Material input to the melt process
    • Mono-material: Single-polymer product (recyclable)
    • Gates/Vents: Mold channels for plastic entry/air exit
    • LCA: Life Cycle Assessment
    • CO₂e: Carbon dioxide equivalent
    • Beam-break: Sensor that counts when a beam is interrupted
