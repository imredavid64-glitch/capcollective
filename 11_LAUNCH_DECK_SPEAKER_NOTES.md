# Cap Collective — Launch Deck Speaker Notes

## OPENING (Slides 1-2)
**Say:** "Sustainability isn't just an idea — it's something we build. Today I want to show you a system that connects what our students *want* to do for the planet with something they can actually *do*, right here, every day."

**Hold up a bottle cap.** "This little thing. It's one of the most dangerous pieces of plastic in the ocean — and one of the most ignored. Only 3% get recycled. We're going to change that."

---

## SLIDE 3-4: WHY CAPS
**Say:** "Caps aren't bottles. Bottles are PET — plastic #1. Caps are HDPE — plastic #2. They can't be recycled together. And because caps are small, they literally fall through the sorting machines at big recycling plants. So they end up in landfill or the ocean. But here — at our school — we can catch them at the source, and HDPE happens to be one of the most valuable, most re-usable plastics there is."

**If asked "Why not just recycle everything?"** → "We do! But caps need separate handling. This program focuses on the highest-value, highest-impact item we can control."

---

## SLIDE 5-8: THE SYSTEM
**Say:** "Here's the bridge. A student walks by, drops a cap in the box. A sensor counts it. The number goes live on our dashboard. Every Friday, we collect, wash, shred, and press. In a few weeks, that cap is a coaster on someone's desk — and the money from that coaster goes to our garden. The loop closes. Nothing is wasted."

**Demo option:** Show the Instructables photos or a live product.

---

## SLIDE 9: EQUIPMENT TIERS
**Say:** "We don't need a factory. For under a thousand dollars, we can start. That buys a heat press, tools, safety gear, and materials for boxes. If we grow, we add a 3D printer and sensors. The point is: we start small and scale with success."

**If asked "What's the total cost?"** → "The Core tier is $500-1,000 to start. We can fund that through a grant or PTA — I have templates ready."

---

## SLIDE 10-15: PRODUCTS & GRADE PROGRAMS
**Say:** "This is what makes it special — it's not one grade. The little ones are 'Cap Detectives' learning to sort and count. The middle schoolers are 'Cap Innovators' programming sensors and building a business. The high schoolers are 'Systems Designers' doing life cycle assessments and writing grants. Everyone has a role."

---

## SLIDE 16: TECHNOLOGY
**Say:** "An $8 sensor turns this from a recycling bin into a data platform. Students see their impact in real time. Research shows that when students see progress, participation triples. This is gamified sustainability."

---

## SLIDE 17: SAFETY
**Say:** "I want to be direct about safety, because it's the first question every administrator asks. Every machine has a written SOP. Every student is certified before touching anything. Temperatures are capped at 200°C. Ventilation is mandatory. PPE is provided. And we have a completed risk assessment with no high residual risks. This is safer than the average woodshop or chem lab."

---

## SLIDE 18-19: TIMELINE & METRICS
**Say:** "Eighteen weeks. Launch, build, process, product, market, scale. By the end of one semester, we're targeting ten thousand caps diverted, eighty percent student participation, and five hundred dollars in revenue. Those aren't aspirational — they're based on what pilot schools actually achieved."

---

## SLIDE 20-23: FUNDING & SUPPORT
**Say:** "We're not asking for a blank check. We've mapped six funding sources, including three grant templates that are ready to submit. And teachers aren't left alone — there's a fifty-page guide, professional development, and a global community of teachers already running this."

## ANTICIPATED Q&A

**Q: Isn't this dangerous for kids?**
A: "No more than an art room or science lab — and we've built the controls to prove it. Certification, supervision ratios, temperature limits, ventilation, PPE, and a full risk register. Every residual risk is rated 'Low.'"

**Q: How much teacher time does this take?**
A: "Two to four hours a week for the lead, and the lessons are pre-written. Most of the operation is student-led — that's the point."

**Q: Where does the money go?**
A: "Back into the program and the school garden. It's designed to become self-funding."

**Q: What if we don't have space?**
A: "We rotate through the science lab or art room one period a week. The press fits on a rolling cart."

**Q: What about fumes and microplastics?**
A: "We only process clean HDPE and PP at low temperatures with active ventilation. We never touch PVC, which is the one that releases toxic gas. Dust is captured at the sander."

**Q: How do we measure success?**
A: "Caps diverted, weight, CO₂e avoided, products made, revenue, and participation — all tracked live in a dashboard we can show to funders."

**Q: Can other schools copy this?**
A: "Yes — it's open source. That's the whole point. We're building a network."

**Q: What if a machine breaks?**
A: "Warranty, spare parts list, and a maintenance schedule. And the program keeps running manually."

**Q: Is there insurance/liability coverage?**
A: "We have a safety MOU and risk register ready for the insurance office to review before launch."
